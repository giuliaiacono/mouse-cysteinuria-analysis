HUMAnN 3.0 functional and metabolic pathway analysis

HUMAnN, like MetaPhlAn, maps reads to a reference database of microbial genomes. The abundance of known gene families (UniRef90) is quantified, and the abundance of pathways of genes is estimated (using the MetaCyc pathways database).

Gene families and pathways can be viewed at the species level, or the community (whole-sample) level - we will focus on community level abundances here. Pathway abundances are proportional to the number of complete “copies” of the pathway in the community. Relative abundance values are shown here are copies-per-millon (CPM), where the abundances for any one sample sum to 1 million. This helps normalize for library size.

Additional gene family and pathway groupings are available but not shown - these are EggNOG Orthologies, EC numbers, Gene Ontology (GO) anntoations, KEGG Orthologies, PFAM protein families and MetaCyc Reactions.

Also recorded are the abundance of UNMAPPED reads (those that failed to map to any reference genome in the database). UNGROUPED or UNINTEGRATED features are those that aren’t present in the grouped database (eg, the UniRef90 family has no annotated reaction in MetaCyc, for instance). Reads that map but aren’t associated with a gene that has a Uniref90 family are labelled UniRef90_unknown.

We can see between ~13% and 43% of reads do not map to the ChocoPhlAn (vOct22) database. This is not unusual, but worth noting this technique does not annotate the entire microbial diversity present in the sample due.

The Abx samples are very different and cluster together (not surprising, they are mostly Lactococcus). If we look at the Het/Homo samples with reveals more detail - the Het samples cluster together (more similar within-group pathway abundances), while the Homo samples are more dispersed. This reflects the higher variance between biological replicates in the Homo samples, as seen in taxonomic abundance analysis previously.

Differential pathway abundance

We investigate pathways with differential abundance in Homo relative to Het, using a linear model with log-transformation of CPM abundances (MaAsLin2 method).

Full HUMAnN/MaAsLin2 association table

  • The qval is the adjusted p-value - rows with qval <= 0.05 are highlighted grey.
  • The coef is the model coef, indicating the strength of the association between the pathway and the group (+ve means pathway is more abudant in Homo)
  • The N column is the total number of data points.
  • The N.not.zero column is the total of non-zero data points.

Significant HUMAnN/MaAsLin2 association table

Here is a filtered version of the same table, showing only associations with a adjusted p-value <= 0.05, and only pathways detected in 90% of samples.

General themes for the identified differential pathways are basic energy metabolism (glycolsis and TCA cycle), pyrimidine and purine degradation, and of particular interest, on cysteine biosynthesis pathway.

PWY-6292 - superpathway of L-cysteine biosynthesis (mammalian) show significant (FDR=0.045) differential pathway abudance, with more complete pathways detected in the Homo microbial communities. (Note that although this is annotated as mammalian, the pathway exists in bacteria and archea).
